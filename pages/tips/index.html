Essential European Train Travel Tips

Make the most of your European rail adventure with these practical tips and insider knowledge.

Before You Travel

📅 Planning Your Journey

  • Book 60-90 days in advance for best prices on high-speed trains
  • Be flexible with dates: Travel Tuesday-Thursday for cheaper fares
  • Check rail operator strike schedules (especially in France and Italy)
  • Download offline maps of major stations (Google Maps, Citymapper)
  • Screenshot tickets and reservations in case of connectivity issues

💰 Money-Saving Strategies

Top 10 Ways to Save Money

  1. Book early: Advance tickets can be 70% cheaper
  2. Night trains: Save on accommodation (sleeper from €29)
  3. Split tickets: Sometimes cheaper to book separate tickets for long journeys
  4. Regional trains: Slower but often much cheaper
  5. Youth cards: Under 27? Get national youth cards (e.g., Carte Jeune in France)
  6. Pack food: Station food is expensive; bring snacks and water
  7. City day passes: Include local trains for exploring regions
  8. Germany's €49 ticket: Unlimited regional travel for €49/month
  9. Avoid Friday/Sunday travel: Peak times = higher prices
  10. Compare booking sites: Prices can vary; check official sites too

🎒 What to Pack

  • Power bank: Not all trains have working outlets
  • Universal adapter: Different plugs across Europe
  • Headphones: For music/entertainment
  • Eye mask & earplugs: For night trains
  • Snacks & water bottle: Cafeteria can be expensive
  • Light jacket: AC can be cold even in summer
  • Pen: For customs forms on international routes

At the Station

⏰ Arrival Time

  • High-speed/International trains: Arrive 30 minutes early
  • Regional trains: 10-15 minutes is usually fine
  • First time at station: Add extra 15 minutes to find your way
  • Eurostar: Arrive 45-60 minutes early (passport control)

🔍 Finding Your Platform

  • Platform numbers often announced 10-20 minutes before departure
  • Check departure boards (Departures/Départs/Abfahrt)
  • Look for train number, not just destination
  • Some stations have different sections (Paris has separate train halls)
  • Download station maps beforehand for large stations

🎫 Ticket Validation

⚠️ When to Validate Tickets

You MUST validate:

  • Regional trains in France (yellow machines)
  • Regional trains in Italy (green/yellow machines)
  • Some regional trains in Spain

NO validation needed:

  • Tickets for specific trains (TGV, ICE, etc.)
  • E-tickets on your phone
  • Rail passes (but show them when asked)
  • German trains (no validation system)

🚻 Station Facilities

  • Toilets: Usually €0.50-1.00 (keep coins!)
  • Luggage storage: €5-10 per day at major stations
  • WiFi: Free at most major stations
  • ATMs: Available but may charge fees
  • Water fountains: Increasingly common in major stations

On the Train

🪑 Finding Your Seat

  • Carriage numbers displayed on platform indicators
  • 1st class usually carriages 1-10, 2nd class 20-30
  • Electronic displays on carriages show reserved seats
  • Red/white cards above seats indicate reservations
  • If no reservation, sit in seats marked "Available" or blank

🧳 Luggage

  • Large bags: Racks at carriage ends or above seats
  • Weight limits: None on most trains (except OUIGO, Ryanair trains)
  • Number of bags: Usually unlimited (be reasonable!)
  • Special items: Bikes need reservation (€10-15), folding bikes usually free
  • Security: Keep valuables with you, not in overhead racks

💡 Luggage Tip

On busy trains, luggage space fills quickly. Board early or keep a small bag with you and store large bags at the carriage end. Don't leave valuables unattended.

🍽️ Food & Drink

  • Bring your own: Allowed on all trains
  • Bistro/Restaurant cars: Available on long-distance trains
  • Prices: Expect to pay 2-3x normal prices
  • Quality: Varies; German ICE bistros generally good
  • Alcohol: Allowed and often sold on board

📱 Staying Connected

  • WiFi: Free on most high-speed trains (quality varies)
  • Power outlets: Usually at seats (1st class) or between seats (2nd class)
  • Mobile signal: Generally good except in tunnels/mountains
  • EU roaming: Use your phone data across EU countries

🤫 Quiet Zones

  • Many trains have designated quiet carriages
  • No phone calls or loud conversations
  • Usually marked with 🤫 or "Quiet Zone" signs
  • Perfect for working or resting

Connections & Transfers

⏱️ Connection Times

  • Same station, same level: 5-10 minutes minimum
  • Large stations (Paris, Munich): 15-20 minutes minimum
  • Station changes: 30-60 minutes (check metro/taxi time)
  • International borders: Add 10 minutes for customs

⚠️ Missed Connections

  • Single ticket: Operator must rebook you on next available train
  • Separate tickets: You're responsible; buy new ticket
  • Rail pass: Just hop on next train (if no reservation needed)
  • Tip: Book connections through one operator when possible

🔄 Changing Stations

Some cities require station changes for certain routes:

  • Paris: 7 major stations; 30-60 min between them
  • London: Multiple stations; Eurostar at St Pancras
  • Brussels: Midi/Zuid for Eurostar, Central for other trains

Safety & Security

🔒 Safety Tips

  • Keep valuables in sight or in secure bag
  • Be aware of pickpockets at busy stations
  • Lock luggage when leaving seat (especially night trains)
  • Don't leave bags unattended
  • Keep passport and tickets on your person
  • Take photo of luggage tag in case bags get lost

🚨 Emergencies

  • Emergency brake in every carriage (only for real emergencies!)
  • Train staff speak English on international routes
  • Emergency number: 112 (works across Europe)
  • Rail operator apps show live train location

Cultural Tips

🌍 Local Customs

  • Greetings: Say hello when sitting next to someone (Germany, Austria)
  • Phone calls: Keep brief and quiet, or use quiet carriage
  • Shoes off: Not expected, but okay on night trains
  • Reserved seats: Honor them; people will ask you to move
  • Tipping: Not expected for train staff

🗣️ Language

  • Staff at major stations usually speak English
  • Learn basic phrases: "Where is...?", "Thank you", "Platform"
  • Google Translate app works offline if you download languages
  • Train announcements usually in local language + English on international routes

First-Time Traveler Checklist

✅ Before Departure

  • ☐ Book tickets 60-90 days in advance
  • ☐ Check passport validity (6 months for some countries)
  • ☐ Download rail operator apps
  • ☐ Screenshot tickets and reservations
  • ☐ Check station locations and transfer times
  • ☐ Note emergency contact numbers
  • ☐ Get travel insurance

✅ At Station

  • ☐ Arrive 20-30 minutes early
  • ☐ Validate ticket if required
  • ☐ Check departure board for platform
  • ☐ Find correct carriage position on platform
  • ☐ Have ticket ready for inspection

✅ On Train

  • ☐ Store large luggage in racks
  • ☐ Keep ticket accessible
  • ☐ Note your stop and arrival time
  • ☐ Keep valuables with you
  • ☐ Enjoy the journey! 🚆
