🇩🇪 Germany Railway Travel Guide

Overview

Germany has one of Europe's most comprehensive and efficient rail networks. Deutsche Bahn (DB) operates over 33,000 km of track, connecting virtually every corner of the country. The ICE (InterCity Express) high-speed network provides fast connections between major cities, while regional trains offer affordable travel throughout the country.

Railway Operator

Deutsche Bahn (DB)

  • Official Website: bahn.com (excellent English version)
  • DB Navigator App: Best for real-time information and booking
  • Customer Service: 24/7 English support available

Types of Trains

ICE (InterCity Express)

  • High-speed trains (up to 300 km/h)
  • Connect major cities across Germany and neighboring countries
  • Modern trains with WiFi, power outlets, bistro car
  • 1st and 2nd class available
  • Reservation optional but recommended

IC/EC (InterCity/EuroCity)

  • Long-distance trains connecting major cities
  • Slower than ICE but still comfortable
  • EC trains continue to neighboring countries
  • No reservation required (except on some routes)

RE (Regional Express)

  • Fast regional trains
  • Skip smaller stations
  • No reservation needed
  • Usually 2nd class only

RB (RegionalBahn)

  • Local trains stopping at all stations
  • Slower but reach smaller towns
  • Perfect for scenic routes

S-Bahn

  • Suburban trains in metropolitan areas
  • Frequent service within cities and suburbs
  • Different fare structure (local transport tickets)

Ticket Types & Pricing

Flexible Tickets (Flexpreis)

  • Valid for any train on your route on the travel day
  • Can take earlier/later trains
  • Fully refundable up to day before travel
  • More expensive but maximum flexibility

Advance Purchase (Sparpreis)

  • Must book specific train
  • Available from €19.90 for long-distance routes
  • Book early (up to 6 months in advance)
  • Limited flexibility - fee to change trains
  • Best value for money

Super Sparpreis

  • Even cheaper than Sparpreis (from €17.90)
  • Very limited availability
  • Non-refundable, non-exchangeable
  • Check early and book fast!

Germany Ticket (Deutschlandticket)

  • €49/month subscription
  • Unlimited travel on regional trains (RE, RB, S-Bahn)
  • Valid on buses, trams, metro throughout Germany
  • NOT valid on ICE/IC trains
  • Perfect for extensive regional travel

🎫 Germany Ticket (€49 Ticket)

Introduced in 2023, this revolutionary monthly pass offers unlimited travel on all regional trains and local transport across Germany for just €49/month. It's subscription-based but can be cancelled monthly. Great value if you're staying in Germany for weeks and want to explore multiple regions!

BahnCard Discounts

  • BahnCard 25: €55.50/year (2nd class) - 25% discount on all tickets
  • BahnCard 50: €229/year (2nd class) - 50% discount on all tickets
  • BahnCard 100: €4,144/year - Unlimited travel on all DB trains
  • Worth it if making 2+ long trips (BC25) or frequent travel (BC50)

Major Train Stations

Berlin Hauptbahnhof (Central Station)

  • Europe's largest two-level train station
  • Main hub for all ICE connections
  • Excellent shopping and dining facilities

Munich Hauptbahnhof

  • One of Germany's busiest stations
  • Gateway to Alps and Austria
  • 32 platforms serving all train types

Frankfurt Hauptbahnhof

  • Major European rail junction
  • Direct trains to Paris, Brussels, Amsterdam
  • Close to Frankfurt Airport

Booking Tips

💰 Money-Saving Tips

  • Book Sparpreis tickets 2-3 months in advance
  • Travel on Tuesdays-Thursdays for better Sparpreis availability
  • Consider regional trains with Germany Ticket (€49) for flexible travel
  • Look for "Sparpreis Europa" for international routes (from €19.90)
  • Early morning and late evening trains often cheaper
  • Group tickets (2-5 people) offer discounts on Sparpreis

Reservations

  • Optional on most trains but recommended for:
    • Friday evenings and Sunday afternoons
    • Holiday periods
    • Long-distance journeys
  • Cost: €4.90 for ICE/IC trains
  • Free with 1st class Flexpreis tickets
  • Can be added to existing tickets via DB app

Travel Tips

  • Trains are generally punctual but delays can happen
  • With Flexpreis, if your train is delayed 20+ minutes, you can take any other train
  • Look for carriage number (Wagen) and seat number (Platz) on reservation
  • 1st class is in carriages numbered 1-10, 2nd class 20-30
  • Quiet zones (Ruhebereich) available in many ICE trains
  • Family areas with play sections for children
  • Extensive luggage racks in all trains
  • Free WiFi on ICE trains (though can be unreliable)

International Connections

Germany is perfectly positioned for European rail travel:

  • To Paris: ICE from Frankfurt/Munich (4-6 hours)
  • To Amsterdam: ICE from Frankfurt/Cologne (2.5-4 hours)
  • To Prague: EC from Berlin/Dresden (4-5 hours)
  • To Vienna: Railjet from Munich (4 hours)
  • To Copenhagen: EC from Hamburg (4.5 hours)
  • To Zurich: ICE from Frankfurt/Munich (4 hours)

DB Navigator App

📱 Essential App Features

  • Real-time train information and delays
  • Platform information
  • Digital tickets
  • Journey planner with connections
  • Live train composition (where each carriage stops)
  • Add reservations to existing tickets
  • Works offline for stored tickets

Accessibility

  • All major stations wheelchair accessible
  • Free assistance service (Mobilitätsservice) - book 24h in advance
  • Wheelchair spaces on all ICE trains
  • Audio announcements for visually impaired

Useful German Phrases

  • Platform: Gleis (pronounced: glice)
  • Train: Zug (pronounced: tsook)
  • Ticket: Fahrkarte (pronounced: far-kar-tuh)
  • Seat: Platz (pronounced: plots)
  • Where is...?: Wo ist...? (pronounced: vo ist)
  • Departure: Abfahrt (pronounced: up-fart)
  • Arrival: Ankunft (pronounced: un-koonft)
